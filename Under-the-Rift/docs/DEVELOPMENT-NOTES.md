# Under the Rift — 1.0

Browser-local esports management game built with plain HTML/CSS and JavaScript ES modules. Serve `dist/` through a static web server; `npm test` runs the deterministic engine tests.

## Original v0.4 release
- Neutral medium-gray interface with tangerine accents, larger supporting text, male player portrait selection and a prominent roster rating.
- Roster/training profile arrows, full composition-link table, training fatigue/Form breakdown, bulk recovery and threshold-based automatic recovery.
- 24 minor traits, 72 club-event scenarios with three choices each. Events cycle without repetition until the deck is exhausted. Six situations within a category share a choice set; effects and risks are shown before selection.
- Club schedule, central next-action controls, draft wire, clickable historical season phases, tournament brackets and game reports even when the club is eliminated.
- Four-matchweek development reports with variable gains and declines.
- Regular-season BO3 with one decision per series; 12% of series have two. Regional playoffs are BO5. MSI has eight participants: the top four regular-season teams from each region, with BO3 quarters and BO5 semis/final. Worlds retains four regional finalists and BO5 semis/final. Each playoff/international game has two decision moments.
- Individual game performance, MVP, series averages and key-play explanations. Three abstract contests decide the game; this is not a full MOBA simulator. Form responds to individual performance relative to ability, regardless of the result.
- Regional/international title celebration screens, with a larger Worlds celebration.

## Existing systems
18 real organizations (8 LCS, 10 LCK), fictional generated players, 321 base names. Four skills on 1–100: Mechanics, Communication, Macro, Teamfighting. Overall = round(50 + average skill / 2), on 50–100. Form is separate, also 50–100. Growth responds to training, experience and manager choices, without predetermined careers.

Each player has two role-specific niches. The engine checks 32 lineup combinations and applies the strongest compatible set of composition links, capped at +4.5 effective rating. Team chemistry includes communication and pair familiarity. Scouting shows estimated ratings, traits, niches and rough development notes; owned profiles expose exact skills and detailed estimates.

Annual three-round academy draft for every club. Contracts have salary, expiry and renewal. Academy prospects train and play abstract development fixtures; call-ups join the bench until selected as starters. Expiring players enter free agency. AI clubs draft, train, trade, recruit and promote academy players through the transfer office. No language system or champion-practice selection.

## Saves
Version 4 careers use IndexedDB to accommodate multi-year game reports, with localStorage fallback and JSON import/export. Existing version-2 local saves are loaded and migrated automatically; their original data is retained. A version-2 match in progress finishes as a legacy single game, then new series rules apply. Earlier results retain their original outcomes and cannot acquire retroactive performance data. Version-1 roster import is still available. Saves remain local to the browser and origin; no cloud synchronization.

## Files
- `dist/data.js`: names, organizations, niches, composition links, traits and match situations.
- `dist/club-events.js`: authored club-event deck and effects.
- `dist/engine.js`: simulation, calendar, training, contracts, academy, events and validation.
- `dist/app.js`, `dist/style.css`: game interface and persistence.
- `dist/assets/`: generated player and champion pixel portrait atlases. Player rendering uses only the male portrait tiles. Champion artwork represents niche classes.
- `tests/engine.test.js`: 21 behavioral tests, including 36 independent full years and a five-year career, save resume/migration, qualification, MVP, Form, recovery and event repetition.

## Custom format and limitations
Single round robin per split: seven LCS rounds and two development byes while LCK completes nine. Top-four regional playoffs. Ties use wins, tied-team head-to-head wins, then fixed seed. This is a custom game calendar, not the official League format. Academy matches use available prospects rather than a role-locked five. Retirement, deep decline and long-term economy balancing remain future work.

## 0.4 — Transfer office

The Market tab now owns recruitment and scouting. Search/filter free agents and club rosters; keep a shortlist, open scout files and compare role incumbents. Main-roster and academy training have separate sections and independent profile navigation. Player names are edited inline through the profile pencil.

After Worlds, the Club next action leads through contract review, a six-round transfer window, deadline resolution, one preseason camp and the next academy draft. Opening the window releases unsigned expiring players and provides board investment. All stages are saveable; existing v2/v3 careers migrate to v4 without resetting results or rosters.

Contract offers specify annual salary, signing bonus, 1–3 additional seasons (renewals) or total seasons (new recruits), and Starter/Rotation/Prospect promises. Agents respond with agreement, counteroffer or rejection. Free agents may choose competing AI bids; player preferences include playing time, winning, security and salary. Bonuses and incremental payroll are reserved while talks are active. A final signing action executes the agreement. Renewals change annual salary immediately and extend existing expiry. During the season, responses arrive after a matchday; preseason and contract review have a dedicated response action.

Trades support up to two players on each side plus cash in either direction. Live valuation reflects ability, age, growth, remaining contract, transfer listing and roster need. Clubs can protect players, reject unbalanced packages or counter with revised cash. Existing player contracts transfer unchanged. Incoming offers expire, can be accepted, declined or countered, and are revalidated on completion. Deals cannot remove a club's final player in a role, double-spend cash, exceed wage budgets or acquire already-moved players. The 22-player recruitment limit excludes academy draft picks. Player release uses a 35% remaining annual-contract settlement and requires role coverage.

AI clubs renew or release contracts, sign free agents, promote strong academy prospects, list surplus players and buy players from other clubs during the window. A chronological transfer wire records moves. Starter and Rotation promises are evaluated every four eligible series, requiring 60% and 25% starts respectively; breaking them reduces trust and Form. Trust affects future salary demands. Preseason camps offer integration, focused skill work or recovery.

Current simplifications: no loans, draft-pick trading, no-trade clauses, individual transfer vetoes or bespoke agent personalities. Counteroffers use deterministic valuation rather than an adversarial negotiating model. AI market activity occurs at transfer rounds, and AI cash is a transfer fund rather than a full club accounting simulation. Financial/growth balance still benefits from longer human playtesting.

Validation: 36 automated behavioral tests cover the full game and market, including multi-year careers, save resume/migration, offer holds, rival bids, counteroffers, atomic trades, expiry, release settlements, promises and deadline rules. Browser checks cover inline renaming, squad separation, recruitment views, agent responses and final signing, a player swap with counteroffered cash, a full season, all six transfer rounds, deadline resolution, camp selection and the 2027 academy draft transition.

## 0.5 — Life inside the club

Club situations doubled from 72 to 144, with 72 new personal, duo and academy scenarios. The deck avoids repeating an event until exhausted and avoids consecutive categories when alternatives remain. Events arrive after 2–3 eligible played series; byes and spectator rounds do not trigger them, and elimination does not lead to another interruption. Existing pending events from eliminated international saves are cleared on load. Personal effects apply to the named player or duo; costs respect contract-offer holds. The decision dialog uses a two-column scene, category icon, portraits, larger typography and explicit effect scope.

Sixteen additional personalities (40 total) affect training, fatigue, recovery, match variance, series pressure and Form. They are drawn for generated players; existing traits remain intact. Empathetic can also be gained through new leadership events.

Club trades are permitted in preseason, the offseason window and the early regular season, closing once either club has completed five series in the current split. Playoffs and internationals remain closed. Each split resets that count. Academy Prospect contracts can be released without a settlement; four training weeks qualify for a one-time development payment of $500 per week plus $500 per gained OVR, capped at $8,000. Service resets on transfer/new signing; payment eligibility cannot be recycled by re-signing the same player. Senior contracts retain the existing settlement even if moved to academy. Releases remain subject to role coverage. The release action is now available in player profiles as well as Market → My squad.

Darker neutral gray surfaces replace the brighter white panels. Eighteen distinct original SVG emblems use the existing team colors across team selection, match previews, Club standings and current/historical Season results. A 950ms match-resolution presentation locks duplicate inputs without changing deterministic outcomes. Result reports show the actual laning, objective and closing-fight edges for each game.

Validation: automated coverage includes event frequency and spectator suppression, all 144 event identities, targeted rewards, held funds, compensation reuse protection, regular-season trade deadlines and personality effects, alongside the existing full-year and multi-year simulations. ## 0.7 — Portrait repair and meaningful match calls

Portrait rendering now uses per-tile SVG viewports from `dist/portrait-crops.js`, measured against each atlas's actual horizontal seams. This fixes the assumption that all generated sheets had exactly equal-height rows. Insets exclude cell-edge contamination and SVG clipping prevents neighboring portraits from bleeding into any portrait size. Source images and player identities are unchanged. The source sheets and supplied screenshot were inspected; all crop windows have been checked for bounds and separation.

Results panels have larger portraits/rows and key moments are open by default. Camel-case trait identifiers are displayed as spaced names while preserving save compatibility. Team marks are filled, layered club-color crests with distinct emblems. Connections moves to Roster. Club's regional rumors panel uses actual competing agent bids, recent transfer ledger entries and listed players, with clearly labeled analyst projections for recruitment needs and academy opportunities. Reading rumors never changes RNG or game state.

Eight match situations expand the decision pool from six to fourteen. Each event has a 10–28% decision weight. The actual result margin now blends the normal contest margin (weighted by one minus the sum of resolved moment weights) with the weighted call outcomes. Safe/control/risky choices expose success probability and the exact positive/negative margin contribution. Safe calls have a higher success probability and smaller downside; risky calls have more upside/downside. Probabilities are distinct from result-score weights, and neither is labeled as game win probability. Recorded outcomes appear in key moments, with immediate feedback after the call. Old completed game results are retained; old in-flight call advantages are converted when a new call resolves.

Validation: 48 existing game tests passed, plus four targeted tests for atlas bounds/non-overlap, displayed call probabilities and stakes, call effects on actual contest margins, and non-mutating region-filtered rumors. Source syntax and diff checks passed. Browser preview remains blocked by automatic approval review, so the visual fixes have not been browser-verified.

Skill-check ideas (not implemented): optional objective-secure timing, Flash reaction, and short minimap-reading challenges. Each should affect only the current moment, cap the bonus, respect player ability and offer a roster-stat-based alternative. A mini-game should never guarantee a series win.

## v0.9 — Career, LPL, and replacement execution moments

- Each interactive moment rolls and saves its mode once: 75% tactical calls, 25% replacement skill check. Calls resolve directly, without a subsequent check. ## v0.11 — Multiple saved careers

Saved careers is available from the header, Settings and new-career setup. New careers and imported JSON files create distinct slots; naming a slot does not rename the manager. The existing single save migrates once into an Original slot without removing either legacy backup key. Each slot includes its manager, club, region, year, stage and last-save timestamp. Inactive slots can be deleted after confirmation; every slot can be renamed or exported.

`career-storage.js` stores separate IndexedDB records in the existing database. Writes are serialized and capture their slot ID and state at invocation. Switching flushes the current save and validates the target before activation. Failed writes block switching/creation instead of discarding progress. Deleted slots reject stale autosaves. If IndexedDB cannot open, separate localStorage records provide the fallback; an established fallback collection remains selected on later startup. There is no fixed slot-count limit, but browser storage capacity still applies. Saves do not sync across devices.
